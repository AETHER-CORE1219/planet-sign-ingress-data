How the Ascendant (rising sign / Lagna) is calculated

The Ascendant is the ecliptic longitude of the eastern horizon at a given instant and place. In Western astrology that longitude is usually expressed in the tropical zodiac; in Jyotish the same geometric point is called the Lagna and is usually expressed in the sidereal zodiac (Lahiri ayanamsa). It is a horizon calculation, not a planet position, so it needs a date, a clock time, a time zone or UTC offset, and the latitude and longitude of the location.

What is computed

  • From the date and time, obtain Greenwich sidereal time and then local sidereal time (add the geographic longitude, east positive).
  • The local sidereal time is the right ascension of the midheaven (MC). The MC’s ecliptic longitude follows from the obliquity of the ecliptic.
  • The Ascendant is the ascending node of the horizon and the ecliptic: the ecliptic degree that is rising at that latitude and local sidereal time.
  • A sidereal (Lahiri) Lagna is the tropical Ascendant minus the Lahiri ayanamsa at that date. Houses are then counted from that degree.

Because the horizon moves through about one degree of the ecliptic in four minutes, a birth time that is off by a few minutes can change the degree and, near a sign boundary, the rising sign itself.
